IBach and the dukeHistory and context · 1708 to 1717→
  1. 1708Johann Sebastian Bach, 23 years old, comes to Weimar as court organist to Duke Wilhelm Ernst of Saxe-Weimar. The duke is pious and strict, and his court runs on fixed routine.
  2. 1714Bach is promoted to Konzertmeister. Every month he has to deliver a new cantata for the castle chapel.
  3. 1716The old Kapellmeister dies. Bach hopes for the post, but the duke gives it to the dead man's son. Bach is also close to the duke's nephew and rival, Ernst August, whose court the duke's musicians are forbidden to serve.
  4. 1717Prince Leopold of Anhalt-Köthen offers Bach the post of Kapellmeister. Bach accepts and asks to leave. The duke says no. Bach insists.
  5. 6 NovThe duke has him locked in the Landrichterstube, the county judge's room, for pushing too stubbornly for his release.
  6. 2 DecAfter almost four weeks Bach is let go, dismissed in disgrace. He leaves Weimar for Köthen.

A legend says he passed the time in the cell working on the Well-Tempered Clavier. Nobody can prove it, but the story stuck to the room.

This project imagines that the room never let go. The duke's routine lives on as a daemon, a program that holds the music in one bar. It is the spirit of the court's routine, not a portrait of the duke.

Bach's Cell

A daemon of ducal routine lives in a narrowing passage in Weimar. A piano piece plays on freely while the passage is empty and while people stay at its wide mouth. When someone walks on into the throat, it gets stuck on whatever bar was playing and repeats it until everyone is back at the mouth or gone. The colour drains the deeper they go, and the music decays the longer it loops. There is no way back: the only way out is the door at the far end of the cell, and the daemon decides how far it opens. The longer people stay in the loop, the more the music distorts and sinks down in pitch, up to an octave. It asks every visitor's name. The last one to enter cannot leave until someone new comes in: if they try, it screams their name and the music distorts further. It remembers only the last person who left, and their bar keeps coming back after they are gone.

      Escape Bach's Cell

      The court's routine lives as a daemon behind a heavy door off the street. Out here the prelude plays freely. Step through the door and it slams shut behind you, and the music gets stuck on whatever bar was playing. It repeats, and decays, and the demons along the walls start calling your name.

      It is a race. Four others go in with you, and all of you run for the same way out. First find a page of music in the dark: the brass grate at the end only rises for someone holding one, and there are only five. The way out is hidden at first, and it only gets smaller: the passage narrows and drops until you crawl in single file. Shove the one in front of you to get past, and take their page if you have none, but every shove costs your mind and draws the demons. Whoever is behind gets held back and hears their name. The demons along the walls want the music too: when one leans in with burning eyes, get out of its reach, or it tears your page away and flings it back into the dark. The demons let everyone go except the last one inside.

      • W A S D or arrow keys to walk, Shift to run. You duck and crawl by yourself where it gets low

      • E at the grate, hands grab you from the dark: press it again and again to break free, or they drag you back
